Change and evidence

Language Change: Evolution or Decay?

Separate observable change from claims about linguistic decline.

1 Interrogate the claim 2 Trace the patterns 3 Model diffusion 4 Diagnose the evidence 5 Reach a conclusion
Learning objectives
  • Distinguish description from prescriptive judgment.
  • Recognize systematic linguistic change.
  • Explain one role of social networks in diffusion.
  • Distinguish change, variation, and endangerment.
  • Connect evidence to a warranted conclusion.

Step 3

Model social diffusion

Test how a fictional variant moves through two simplified network structures.

Transparent teaching model

Orange speakers use a fictional new form. In each round, another speaker adopts it when the selected share of their contacts already uses it. This is an illustration—not a forecast of any community.

0 of 24speakers after the final round

What does this model leave out?

Real speakers differ in identity, mobility, prestige, linguistic repertoire, relationships, and willingness to adopt a form. Contact also changes over time.

Interpretation: A model can isolate a mechanism without reproducing the full social world. Its simplification limits the conclusion.
